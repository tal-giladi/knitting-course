# Left-handed and Continental knitting

You do not need to be left-handed to finish this course, and being left-handed does not limit what
you can make. Knitting uses both hands: one holds the fabric, one works the yarn. Almost every
left-handed knitter learns the standard method without difficulty, and most never think about it
again.

This page collects all three options in one place. Every technique lesson also has a
`Left-handed and Continental` subsection, so you never have to translate a whole lesson yourself.

## The three options

| | Where the yarn is held | How it reaches the needle | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **English** (*throwing*) | Right hand | You throw it over the right index finger to create slack, then catch it | Most learners; quick to pick up; a little faster for some hands |
| **Continental** (*picking*) | Left hand, under tension from the fingers | You pick the yarn up with the needle tip, no slack needed | Many left-handers; left-handers find the yarn already where they want it; fewer motions per stitch |
| **Mirror knitting** | Either | The work hangs off the **right** needle and stitches are worked right to left | Left-handers who find the standard method genuinely awkward. More on it below. |

You can mix them freely. Plenty of knitters throw for the body and pick for colourwork, or use
English until ribbing and then switch. The stitches are identical; only the path of the yarn
changes.

> [!TIP]
> The choice that matters is **English versus Continental**, and it is worth trying both for ten
> minutes each. Pick the one that is less tiring, not the one that looks most traditional. You can
> always change later; nothing you knit is affected by which method made it.

## Continental knitting in three rules

1. **Wrap the yarn around your left fingers** so it is lightly tensioned. Too loose and the fabric
   gets sloppy; too tight and your hand cramps within ten minutes. If your fingers ache, the wrap
   is too tight.
2. **Pick the yarn up with the needle tip** from front to back, scoop, and slide the new loop off —
   no throwing, no slack.
3. **Purls are the adjustment.** Two options:
   - **Flick**: hold the yarn where it is and flick it over the front of the needle, the way a
     right-hander throws.
   - **Purl as if knitting**: bring the yarn *behind* the needle and scoop it through the loop from
     the back, so a purl and a knit use the same hand motion. This is the fastest continental
     method and many knitters prefer it. In patterns, this is written as "purl as if knitting" or
     purl with the yarn in back.

## Mirror knitting

Mirror knitting means the work hangs off the right needle and you work stitches from the right
needle to the left, in the opposite direction to the standard method. The right needle does the
knitting; the left needle receives the stitches.

**Most people should not bother.** It is genuinely useful only if you find the standard method
uncomfortable after a fair attempt. Before committing, try Continental for a while — for a lot of
left-handers it solves the same problem with no pattern consequences at all.

If you do mirror, three things change, and they are the only three:

| | Standard | Mirrored |
|---|---|---|
| **Chart and written-pattern direction** | Worked as written, usually right side row first | Reversed: the row that is read first is worked last, and row order runs the other way |
| **Decrease slant** | k2tog leans right, ssk leans left | k2tog leans left, ssk leans right |
| **Picking up stitches** | Centre outward, or edge inward, whichever the pattern says | Whichever you did becomes the other; the *count* is always the same |

Note that the **counts never change** and the fabric is the same fabric. Only the direction you
read and the direction a decrease leans are mirrored. This is also why the diagrams in this course
are drawn right-handed: mirror them by flipping the image, and the numbers still match.

## Translation table for this course's techniques

Every technique the projects use, in both directions. "Standard" is what the lessons say and what
the patterns are written in; "mirrored" is what you do instead.

| Technique | Standard | Mirrored |
|---|---|---|
| Long-tail cast on | Hold tail in left, wrap with right needle | Identical; the cast-on edge has no direction |
| Knit | Left needle holds, right needle works | Right needle holds, left needle receives |
| Purl | Yarn in front, right needle works from back | Yarn in back, right needle works from front |
| Bind off | Knit 2, pass 1 to the left needle, repeat | Identical — the bind-off is symmetric |
| Joining a new ball | Overlap at the edge, away from the row you are on | Identical |
| Weaving in ends | Run the tail along the inside of a row | Identical — weave in the inside either way |
| Joining the round | Arrange stitches so the right side faces out | Identical; check the cast-on edge is not twisted |
| 1x1 rib in the round | Alternate k, p, marker between rounds | Identical; keep the marker between a k and a p |
| k2tog | Knit 2 together; leans right | Leans left |
| ssk | Slips lean left | Lean right |
| p2tog | Leans left | Leans right |
| kfb | One stitch into the front loop, then the back | Identical; the stitch leans the other way |
| M1L / M1R | Use left, use right | Swap the two names; the *count* is what matters |
| K / P decreases for hat crown | Decreases evenly spaced around the round | Identical spacing; slants mirror |
| Picking up underarm stitches | Along the picked-up edge, every stitch | Identical; the row of picked-up stitches faces the other way |
| Raglan increases | One increase on each side of each of 4 lines, 8 per round | Identical: still 8 per round, still 4 lines |
| Reading a chart | RS row first, right to left, as written | Reverse the reading direction of every row |
| "Work in pattern" | As established | As established, but the repeat starts at the other end |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Whatever method you use, **write it on the first line of your own notes** and tell nobody else
> about it if you do not want advice. The most common question on any knitting forum is "why does
> my k2tog lean the wrong way", and for mirror knitters it is leaning the correct way for them.

## Tips specific to left-handers

- **Do not hold the needle like a right-hander just to be a left-hander.** The needle sits where
  it is comfortable; only the yarn path needs a decision.
- **Avoid tight grip as a workaround.** Many left-handers knit with a tighter grip because they
  are compensating for an awkward method. The fix is a different method, not a tighter hand — and
  tight hands are how hands get hurt. See lesson 14.1.
- **Take breaks before you feel you need them**, not after. Lesson 14.1 covers comfort properly.
- **A crochet hook helps everyone.** Making dropped stitches is much easier with one, and it is a
  left-hander's natural grip, not a workaround.
