# Yarn weights and needles

Two numbers control how a fabric behaves: the **thickness of the yarn** and the **thickness of the
needle** you use with it. This page is the reference table for both, so you can buy the right thing
and substitute the right thing.

## The weight system

Yarn is sold in weight categories. The numbers are not a measurement — 4-ply does not mean
"weight 4" — but the categories are standardised, so a yarn labelled *worsted* in one country is a
comparable yarn in another.

The gauge column is stockinette — knit one row, purl the next — on 4" (10 cm), and it is only
reached if you use a needle in the range shown.

| Weight | Name (US / UK) | Knitting gauge, sts per 4" (10 cm) | Metric needle | US needle | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Lace / 2-ply | 32-40 | 1.75-2.25 mm | 00-2 | shawls, doilies |
| 1 | Fingering / 3-ply | 28-32 | 2.25-3 mm | 1-3 | socks, fine sweaters |
| 2 | Sport / 4-ply | 22-26 | 3.25-3.75 mm | 3-5 | lightweight sweaters |
| 3 | DK | 19-21 | 3.75-4.5 mm | 5-7 | sweaters, hats, shawls |
| 4 | Worsted / Aran | 16-18 | 4.5-5.5 mm | 7-9 | **this course**, sweaters, scarves |
| 5 | Aran / Chunky | 12-14 | 5.5-6.5 mm | 9-11 | sweaters, blankets |
| 6 | Bulky | 8-11 | 6.5-9 mm | 11-14 | blankets, scarves |
| 7 | Super bulky | 5-6 | 9-20 mm | 15-19 | blankets |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The UK names are shifted by one relative to the US names, and this trips everybody up. UK
> *Aran* is US *worsted*; UK *Chunky* is US *bulky*. If a British pattern says "Aran", it wants the
> same yarn as an American pattern saying "worsted".

## Needle sizes

Knitting needle sizes are **not** the same in metric and US, and there is no formula — the two
systems were set independently. Learn the rows you need rather than converting.

| Metric | US | UK | Metric | US | UK |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 mm | 0 | 14 | 4.0 mm | 6 | 11 |
| 2.25 mm | 1 | 13 | 4.5 mm | 7 | 10 |
| 2.75 mm | 2 | 12 | **5.0 mm** | **8** | **6** |
| 3.0 mm | 3 | 11 | 5.5 mm | 9 | 5 |
| 3.25 mm | 4 | 10 | 6.0 mm | 10 | 4 |
| 3.5 mm | 4-5 | 9 | 6.5 mm | 11 | 3 |
| 3.75 mm | 5 | 8 | 7.0 mm | 12 | 2 |
| | | | 8.0 mm | 14 | 1 |
| | | | 9.0 mm | 15 | 00 |
| | | | 10.0 mm | 16 | 000 |

**5.0 mm is US 8, and it is the one this course uses.** The same needle is size 6 in the UK system.

Circular needles are sold by the **cable length**, not only by the tip: 40 cm (16"), 60 cm (24"),
80 cm (32"), 100 cm (40"). The cable length limits the size of the tube you can knit in the round
comfortably. An 80 cm (32") cable is a good all-round choice: long enough for a hat or a sweater
body, short enough to be manageable.

## Choosing a needle to hit a gauge

Needle size and gauge move in the same direction: **a bigger needle makes a looser fabric with
fewer stitches in the same width.** This is the only lever you have for changing the fabric without
changing the yarn, and it is how you make a yarn work at a gauge it was not spun for.

- Gauge too tight (more stitches than the pattern wants)? Go up one needle size.
- Gauge too loose (fewer stitches than wanted)? Go down one needle size.
- Needles come in fractional sizes, so you can move 0.25 mm (US 1) at a time once you are close.

> [!TIP]
> Change the needle, not the tension. A fabric knitted too tightly to reach a gauge is a fabric
> you will be fighting all the way to the end, and it will not block to the right size.

## How much yarn

A ball or skein label gives a **yardage or metreage**, and the pattern tells you what the finished
item needs. To work out what you have to buy:

$$\text{balls needed} = \left\lceil \frac{\text{yardage the pattern needs}}{\text{yardage on one ball}} \right\rceil$$

Round **up**, always, and remember these three deductions:

- **Swatches come out of the same ball.** A generous gauge swatch, knitted and washed, can use
  15-20 m. Count it.
- **Scarf, hat and sweater are not the same shape.** A sweater has far more surface area than a
  hat of similar circumference, and the neck, hem and sleeves add more.
- **Weaving in ends, and a second ball for a long scarf**, need a few metres of slack.

| Project | Yarn needed |
|---|---|
| Scarf, 20 cm x 180 cm, worsted weight | 200-300 m |
| Hat, adult, worsted weight | 100-150 m |
| Sweater, adult, worsted weight | 800-1500 m, depending on size |

## Dye lots

A **dye lot** is a batch of yarn dyed together, printed as a number on the label. Two balls with
different dye lots are very slightly different colours, and a knitted fabric shows the boundary as
a visible line.

For anything larger than a hat, buy enough yarn for the whole project **in one lot**, or at least
alternate the lots as you knit. See [06.5](../lessons/module-06/lesson-05.md) for how to substitute
safely.
