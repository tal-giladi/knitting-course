## 1. One-paragraph pitch

A free-standing, principle-first knitting course for complete beginners. With one ball of
yarn and one pair of needles the learner knits their first stitch in lesson 2, and by the end
has made three things they will actually wear: a scarf, a hat and a simple top-down sweater.
Unlike most beginner courses, which hand over a pattern and a video, every lesson explains
**why** the stitches behave the way they do — why stockinette curls and ribbing pulls in, why
gauge decides whether a sweater fits, why a hat crown closes in eight wedges, why wool blocks
and cotton does not. Learners are taught to **read their own knitting**, diagnose and fix
mistakes without panic, read patterns and charts, and finally design their own hat and adapt
a pattern to their body and their yarn. Each lesson is a short illustrated text with numbered
steps, a diagram for every motion and a short video, with left-handed guidance throughout.

## 2. Target learner and outcome

**Learner (public audience):** an adult (or teenager) who has never knitted, or who learned
once as a child and forgot. They want to make real, wearable things, and they want to
understand the craft well enough to stop depending on step-by-step videos.

**Assumed:** nothing about knitting. Basic arithmetic (multiplying and dividing with a
calculator) for gauge and sizing. A tape measure.

**Not assumed:** any craft experience, crochet, sewing, a local yarn shop, or a teacher
nearby.

**Kit (the brief: only yarn and needles):**

| Item | Proposal | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Needles | One 5 mm (US 8) circular needle, 80 cm (32") cable | Knits the scarf flat, the hat and sleeves by magic loop, the sweater body in the round. One tool for the whole course (see §12, question 3). |
| Yarn | Smooth, light-coloured, worsted/aran-weight wool or wool blend | Stitches are easy to see and fix; wool forgives uneven tension and blocks well. |
| Small extras | Scissors, a tapestry needle, a tape measure; stitch markers (or loops of scrap yarn) | Everyday household items or near-free; the course shows substitutes. |
| Optional | A crochet hook about the needle size | Makes fixing dropped stitches easier; the course also teaches fixing with the needle alone. |

Yarn totals per project (estimates, to be confirmed when the patterns are written and
test-knitted): scarf ~200-300 m, hat ~100-150 m, sweater ~800-1 500 m depending on size.

**Estimated time:** reading ~10-12 h; knitting ~50-80 h in total (scarf ~8-12 h, hat ~5-8 h,
sweater ~30-50 h, swatches and practice ~8-10 h). Stated as typical ranges, never promises.

**Outcome — by the end the learner can:**

1. Cast on, knit, purl, bind off, and work flat and in the round, with even tension.
2. Explain how a knitted fabric is built (loops, columns, rows, the two faces of a stitch) and
   predict how knit/purl combinations behave (curl, lie flat, pull in, texture).
3. Knit and measure a proper swatch, and use gauge to predict and control the size of anything
   they knit.
4. Choose and substitute yarn by weight, fibre and yardage, and predict how the fabric will
   drape, stretch, block and wear.
5. Read a written pattern, its abbreviations, schematics and charts, choose a size using ease,
   and recognise when a pattern contains an error.
6. Read their own knitting: identify stitches and rows, count, spot a mistake several rows
   down, and fix it (tink, ladder down, frog to a lifeline).
7. Knit a scarf, a hat and a top-down raglan sweater that fits.
8. Design a simple hat from their own gauge, adapt a pattern's length, width or yarn, and
   write a pattern someone else can follow.

### Intended differentiator

The survey above is not exhaustive. Among what we found, free resources are either technique
libraries (every stitch on video, no path) or pattern collections (a path, but only steps to
follow). Our intended differentiator is the combination of:

1. **Principle first:** every lesson has a `## Why it works` section and every project is
   built around one reusable principle (§5), so the learner leaves able to knit without us.
2. **Reading and fixing your own knitting** taught early (Module 5) and practised in every
   later lesson (`## Check your work` and `## Fixing common mistakes`).
3. **Gauge-driven projects:** the hat and sweater are written as "your gauge, your size"
   formulas with worked tables, not as one fixed stitch count.
4. **A path to designing:** the course ends with the learner designing a hat and adapting a
   pattern, with a pattern-writing template.
5. **One-needle kit, text + diagram + video,** and real left-handed support.

## 4. The core problem: fast, visible progress with a slow craft

Knitting is slow, and beginners quit when the first project drags or when a mistake seems
unfixable. Requirements:

- **First stitch in lesson 2** (~30 min in): no long theory up front.
- **Something finished early:** the scarf is started in Module 4 (end of week 1-2 for most
  learners). Before that, every lesson produces a small, labelled **practice swatch**.
- **A swatch book:** each practice swatch is ~20 stitches wide and 10-15 cm tall, tagged with a
  paper label (stitch pattern, needle, yarn). Swatches are kept, not thrown away: by Module 6
  the learner has a physical reference library of fabrics and gauges. Optional: sew them into
  a patchwork blanket at the end.
- **Mistakes as lessons:** Module 5 teaches fixing before the first long project, and practice
  swatches include deliberate mistakes to find and fix ("drop a stitch on purpose, ladder
  down 4 rows, pick it back up").
- **Progress photos:** each project page has checkpoints ("photograph your hat after the
  brim") and a "what good looks like" photo or diagram at each checkpoint.
- **Try on as you go:** the top-down sweater is tried on at the underarm and at every 10 cm of
  body and sleeve, so fit problems are caught early.

### 4.1 Diagrams and video

- **Diagrams (every motion, every fabric structure):** SVG files under `assets/`, drawn in
  one consistent style: right needle / left needle / working yarn in fixed, labelled colours,
  numbered arrows for motion, and every diagram readable without colour (labels and line
  styles carry the meaning). Structure diagrams show loops magnified (how a knit and a purl
  interlock; why k2tog leans right). Each image has meaningful alt text that describes the
  motion in words.
- **Video:** one short (1-5 min) video per technique, linked as a YouTube URL on its own line
  (embedded by the Academy). Options for the source are an open decision (§12, question 1).
  Video never carries information that the text and diagrams do not also carry.
- **Close-up photos** for reading knitting ("this is a dropped stitch", "this is a twisted
  stitch"). Source is part of the same open decision.

### 4.2 Optional simulations (educational models)

Plain HTML/JS under `simulations/`, per section 7 of the guidelines. Candidates, in priority
order (whether to build any is §12, question 6):

1. **Gauge and size calculator:** stitches per 10 cm + target size → cast-on count, and the
   reverse; shows what a half-stitch gauge error does to a sweater's chest.
2. **Hat designer:** gauge + head circumference + ease → cast-on rounded to the crown repeat,
   brim, body length and crown-decrease table.
3. **Chart reader:** a small chart where the learner steps through rows (flat vs in the round)
   and sees which stitch each square means and which direction to read.

The course must be complete without them (every calculation also appears as a worked table and
a formula in the lesson text).

## 5. The projects and the principle each one teaches

Projects are **original patterns written for this course** (no copied patterns), each as a
project page under `projects/`. Every project page has a `## The principle` section, and the
project's lessons build towards it.

| Project | What the learner makes | Principle it teaches (reusable) | Techniques |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 Scarf (Module 4) | A reversible scarf that lies flat (garter or a garter/rib/seed choice) | **Fabric structure decides behaviour.** Knit and purl stitches pull the fabric in opposite directions; balanced patterns (garter, rib, seed) lie flat, unbalanced ones (stockinette) curl. Choose the stitch pattern for the job. | Cast on, knit, purl, selvedges, joining yarn, bind off, weaving ends, blocking |
| P2 Hat (Module 9) | A ribbed-brim beanie sized to the learner's own head from their own gauge | **Gauge × measurement = stitch count, and ease decides fit.** Circumference is stitches ÷ gauge; negative ease for a snug hat; a crown closes a circle by removing stitches evenly in wedges. | Knitting in the round, magic loop, ribbing, decreases, closing the top |
| P3 Sweater (Module 12) | A top-down raglan pullover, seamless, sized from the learner's measurements | **A garment is geometry plus ease.** Raglan lines add 8 stitches every other round because four lines each grow on both sides; the yoke depth, chest and sleeve widths come from a schematic and the learner's gauge; trying on as you go replaces guesswork. | Neckband, raglan increases, holding stitches, underarm cast-on and pick-up, sleeve decreases, garment blocking |
| P4 Design your own (Module 13, capstone) | The learner's own hat (or cowl/scarf), designed from a swatch and written up as a pattern | **You can design from principles.** Swatch → gauge → measurements → stitch counts → a written pattern; test-knit it and correct it. | Everything above, pattern writing |

The sweater pattern is written as formulas plus a size table (e.g. chest 80-150 cm in steps)
for the course's reference gauge, and a "your gauge" worksheet for other gauges.

## 6. Assessment design

Per section 5 of the guidelines:

- **Every graded question is multiple choice**, in `.quiz.yaml` files: exactly 4 options, one
  correct, a teaching `explanation`, positions spread across 0-3, no all/none of the above.
- **Lesson quizzes:** 3-5 questions per lesson, testing the principle, not memorised steps
  (e.g. "Your stockinette scarf rolls into a tube. What change fixes it?").
- **Module quizzes:** 8-10 scenario questions per module, focused on diagnosis and reasoning:
  describing a fabric or a situation in words and asking what happened, what to do next, or
  what will result. Examples of scenario types:
  - Diagnosis: "There is a vertical ladder of horizontal bars running down three rows below
    your needle. What happened and what is the quickest fix?"
  - Prediction: "Your swatch has 20 stitches per 10 cm; the pattern says 18. If you follow the
    pattern's stitch counts, the 100 cm chest will measure about…"
  - Choice: "Which yarn will give a sweater that keeps its shape after washing?"
  - Pattern reading: "The chart row is worked in the round. Which direction do you read it?"
- Wrong options are the mistakes beginners really make (knitting into the back loop by
  accident, measuring an unblocked swatch, reading every chart row right to left when working
  flat), never jokes.
- Questions are text-based and must stand alone; if a question needs a picture, the picture is
  described in words (whether the importer supports images in quizzes is not assumed).
- **Practical projects** (scarf, hat, sweater, design-your-own) are project pages with
  checkpoints and a self-check rubric (below). They are not graded quizzes; each project's
  module still has its multiple-choice module quiz on the same material, per the guidelines.

### 6.1 Project self-check rubric (on each project page)

| Criterion | What "meets the bar" looks like |
|---|---|
| Measurements | Finished size within ~5% of the planned size (hat, sweater) |
| Gauge | Swatch knitted, washed, measured; gauge recorded on the project page |
| Fabric | Even tension; no unintended holes, twisted or dropped stitches |
| Edges and finishing | Tidy selvedges, stretchy enough bind-off/cast-on, ends woven in invisibly, blocked |
| Principle | The learner can explain in their project notes why the fabric/shape/fit came out the way it did, and what they would change next time |

No code. `labs/module-NN/` holds printable material for the module (swatch cards, the hat
worksheet, the sweater size table and worksheet, printable charts), in markdown or SVG so the
module zip is useful offline. Whether to include printable PDFs is §12, question 7.

## 8. Public learning experience and accessibility

### 8.1 Positioning

The course home page says plainly: for complete beginners; three wearable projects; what to
buy (one needle, one yarn, household extras) and roughly what it costs; how long it takes;
and that the course teaches why, so the learner can go on alone.

### 8.2 Left-handed knitters

- Knitting uses both hands; most left-handed people learn the standard method successfully.
  Lesson 01.4 says this plainly and presents the options without pushing one.
- **Continental (picking)** holds the yarn in the left hand and is often comfortable for
  left-handers; every technique lesson has a `### Left-handed and Continental` subsection
  (inside `## Step by step`) with the Continental version.
- **Mirror knitting** (working from the right needle to the left) is described in
  `references/left-handed.md` with its main consequence: written patterns and chart
  directions must be mirrored, and decreases lean the other way. Mirror knitters get a
  translation table for every technique used in the projects.
- Diagrams are drawn so that a mirrored version can be produced from the same source file
  (whether to publish mirrored diagrams is §12, question 5).

### 8.3 Diagrams, video and text

- Every technique has: numbered written steps, one diagram per step (or a combined step
  diagram), and a video link. Text + diagrams are complete on their own; video is support.
- Every image has alt text that describes the motion or structure in words.
- Diagrams never rely on colour alone: needles, yarn and arrows are also distinguished by
  labels, line style and numbering. High contrast; works in light and dark themes.
- Videos: captions required; close-up framing; slow speed; ideally both English and
  Continental where it matters.

### 8.4 Language and units

- English, per the guidelines. Metric first with US equivalents in brackets (needles:
  "5 mm (US 8)"; lengths "10 cm (4")").
- US terms by default ("bind off", "gauge") with UK terms in the glossary and noted in the
  first lesson that uses them.

### 8.5 Physical comfort

- Lesson 14.1 covers comfort, but the first practice lessons already remind learners to take
  breaks and relax their grip. The risk notice in `course-details.md` is short and mild.

## 9. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Learners quit on the first long project | Swatch-per-lesson progress; scarf kept to a sensible length (a shorter "cowl" option); fixing taught before long projects |
| Mistakes feel unfixable | Module 5 before the hat and sweater; deliberate-mistake exercises; lifelines as a habit from the scarf onward |
| Text cannot show hand motions well | One diagram per step, a video per technique, close-up photos; tested on real beginners (§11) |
| Gauge is skipped, so the sweater doesn't fit | Gauge taught as the central principle (Module 6); hat and sweater are gauge-driven formulas; try-on checkpoints |
| Sweater is too big a step | Top-down seamless raglan, tried on as you go; hat first teaches the round, decreasing and ease; sweater split into six lessons with checkpoints |
| Pattern errors in our own patterns | Every pattern test-knitted in at least two sizes before publishing (§10, phase 3); worked stitch-count tables; errata page |
| Copyright | All patterns, diagrams and text original; no copied patterns or book diagrams; external videos only as links; external patterns only as links for further practice |
| Video production effort | Open decision (§12, question 1); the course is complete without own video |
| Yarn availability differs by country | Specify yarn by weight, fibre and gauge, never by brand; substitution taught in 06.5 |
| Left-handed learners left out | Continental subsection in every technique lesson; mirror knitting reference with translation tables |
| Hand or wrist strain | Comfort reminders from the first practice lessons; lesson 14.1; mild risk notice |
| Quizzes cannot see the learner's knitting | Quizzes test reasoning via described scenarios; the practical work is judged by the project self-check rubric |

## 11. Success criteria

- A complete beginner, with only the course, knits a scarf that lies flat, a hat that fits
  their head, and a sweater within ~5% of the planned chest measurement.
- A beginner tester reaches the first finished swatch in under an hour and the finished scarf
  within about two weeks of casting on at a few sessions a week.
- Every lesson has a `## Why it works` section, and every project page states its principle.
- Every technique lesson has numbered steps, diagrams with alt text, a Continental/left-handed
  subsection and a video link.
- Learners can pass the module quizzes' diagnosis questions (the reasoning, not recall, is the
  point of the quizzes).
- All three course patterns have been test-knitted in at least two sizes with no open errata.
- The Academy dry-run import (`npm run check-course`) reports `0 problems`; the first real
  import needs no edits.

## 15. Lesson front-matter conventions

Per section 4 of the guidelines. Course-specific notes:

- `minutes`: reading time (typically 8-15).
- `practice_minutes`: hands-on knitting time for the lesson's practice swatch (0 only for
  pure reading lessons such as 01.1 or 10.1). Project lessons state a range in the text; the

## 16. Lesson template

Every lesson uses this fixed set of `##` sections, in this order (per the guidelines: one
fixed set for the whole course, `###` inside sections, no quiz/knowledge-check section — the
quiz lives only in the `.quiz.yaml`). A section that does not apply to a lesson stays, with one
line saying so (e.g. "No new technique in this lesson").

```markdown
---
(front-matter per §15)
---

# NN.M · Title

One plain intro paragraph: what the learner will do and why it matters.

## What you need
Yarn, needle, extras; the previous swatch if the lesson continues it.

## Why it works
The principle behind the lesson: how the stitches, fabric, fibre or geometry make this
happen. A structure diagram where it helps. This is the lasting value; it is never skipped.

## Step by step
Numbered steps, one diagram per step (alt text describes the motion), the video link on its
own line.
### Left-handed and Continental
The same technique with the yarn in the left hand, and a note for mirror knitters.

## Check your work
What good looks like (diagram/photo), how to read it in the fabric, what to count.

## Fixing common mistakes
The two or three mistakes beginners actually make here: symptom → cause → fix.

## Practice
The practice swatch or the project step for this lesson, with a clear finish line and a
swatch-card or checkpoint to fill in.

## Tips from experienced knitters
Practical habits and shortcuts related to this lesson.

## Recap
Three to five bullets, ending with the one principle to remember and where it comes back
later in the course.
```

Project pages (`projects/*.md`) use their own fixed set: `## The principle`, `## What you
need`, `## Sizes and measurements`, `## Gauge`, `## Pattern`, `## Checkpoints`,
`## Self-check`, `## Make it your own`.
