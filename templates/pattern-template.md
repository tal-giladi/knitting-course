# Pattern template — (name of the pattern)

The structure used by this course's own patterns, and the one you use to write yours in Module 13.
A pattern is a set of instructions someone else can follow without asking you a single question.
Copy this, delete what does not apply, and fill it in.

> [!TIP]
> Write the pattern for the knitter you were six months ago. They know how to knit, they have not
> worked out your abbreviations, and they are not going to guess. If you have to explain something
> in conversation, it needs explaining in the pattern.

---

## Name

Something specific. "Ribbed beanie" is not enough to search for; "Tall ribbed beanie, 3 sizes" is.

## Intro

One short paragraph: what it is, how hard it is, and what someone should already know.

- **Skill level:** (beginner / intermediate — and say what that means here)
- **Sizes:** (e.g. S–XL, with finished measurements)
- **Time:** (a range, and what dominates it — "10 hours, of which 8 are the sleeves")

## Abbreviations

## Construction notes

The parts that are not obvious from the row-by-row text: the neckband, the underarm, the
decrease schedule, anything where a knitter will stop and think.

## Instructions

Repeat this block per size where sizes differ. **State the stitch count after every row or round** —
it is the cheapest error check a knitter has, and it should never be missing.

```text
**Neckband**

With RS facing, cast on 44 sts (40, 48, 56) and join to work in the round, being careful not to
twist. Place a marker to denote the beginning of the round.
Work 8 rounds of 1x1 rib (RS rounds: k1, *p1; WS rounds: p1, *k1).
```

Follow the conventions this course uses:

- `RS` and `WS` are named on the first mention of a face, not every row.
- Repetition is written with `*…*` and `[ ] 4 times`, never spelled out.
- The final count goes in brackets at the end of the line, in size order S, M, L, XL.
- Changes in size are written in brackets, never as separate paragraphs.

## Chart

```text
Chart: 8-st repeat, worked in the round
RS:  *k1, p1* — repeat from * to the end of the round
```

Give the key: what each square means, which side the chart is read from, and what a no-stitch square
is.

## Finishing

Bind off, weave in ends, block, and say **how** to block — for this project, specifically. "Block
to measurements" is not enough; "steam or wet-block flat to the schematic measurements and let dry
completely" is.

## Notes

- Optional variations and how to change them (length, colour, yarn).
- What to do if you are between sizes, and which one to choose.
- Anything that surprised you while test-knitting.
