# Knitting from Zero

Learn to knit from your very first stitch to a scarf, a hat and a sweater that fits — and
understand *why* knitting works, so you can fix mistakes, read any pattern and design on your own.
Everything you need is one ball of yarn, one circular needle and about ten hours of reading.

## What you will make

| Project | Module | The principle it teaches you |
|---|---|---|
| A reversible scarf that lies flat | 4 | Fabric structure decides behaviour: knit and purl pull in opposite directions, and balanced patterns lie flat |
| A hat that fits *your* head | 9 | Gauge × measurement = stitch count, and ease decides fit |
| A seamless top-down raglan sweater that fits *you* | 12 | A garment is geometry plus ease |
| A hat you design yourself, written up as a pattern | 13 | You can design from principles |

## Who it is for

A complete beginner — adult or teenager — who has never knitted, or who learned once as a child and
forgot. You do not need any craft experience, and you do not need a local yarn shop or a teacher
next to you. If you have ever wanted to make something you can wear, this is the course.

**You are assumed to know:** arithmetic with a calculator, how to measure something with a tape
measure, and how to use scissors.

**You are not assumed to know:** anything about knitting, yarn, fibres or patterns. Left-handed
knitters are supported throughout: every technique lesson has a Continental and mirror-knitting
version, and there is a [reference page](references/left-handed.md) that collects them all.

## What you need

| | |
|---|---|
| Circular needle | One 5 mm (US 8) circular needle with an 80 cm (32") cable. One tool for the whole course. |
| Yarn | Smooth, light-coloured, worsted-weight (aran) wool or wool blend. One ball is enough to start. |
| Extras | Scissors, a tapestry needle (or a blunt sewing needle), a tape measure, and two stitch markers (or two small loops of scrap yarn). |
| Useful later | A crochet hook about the size of your needle makes fixing dropped stitches much easier. Optional — every fix is also shown with the knitting needle alone. |

Total cost is roughly the price of one ball of yarn and one needle, plus a few small household
items. Exact prices vary by country; every yarn requirement in this course is written as a **weight,
a fibre and a gauge**, never a brand, so you can buy whatever is available to you.

## How the course works

- **Every lesson has a `Why it works` section.** This is the part that stays useful after the
  course ends. It explains the structure, the fibre, the geometry or the maths behind the technique.
- **Every lesson ends with something knitted.** A small, labelled practice swatch — about 20
  stitches wide — so you finish something in every sitting and can compare fabrics later. Keep them.
- **Every technique has a diagram for each step**, with text that describes the motion, and a short
  captioned video. The text and diagrams are complete on their own; video is support, not the lesson.
- **You learn to read your own knitting** in Module 5, before the long projects, so a mistake in
  week three is a two-minute fix rather than a crisis.
- **Quizzes test reasoning, not memory.** You will be shown a fabric or a situation and asked what
  happened and what to do next.

## Structure

Six parts, 14 modules, 66 lessons, plus four projects.

| Part | Modules | |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — First stitches | 1-4 | Tools and yarn, the knit stitch, the purl stitch, and your first project: the scarf |
| 2 — Understanding your knitting | 5-7 | Reading and fixing your work, gauge, yarn weight and fibre, shaping |
| 3 — Knitting in the round | 8-9 | Seamless knitting, and your second project: the hat |
| 4 — Patterns and charts | 10 | Reading any pattern, and knowing when one is wrong |
| 5 — The sweater | 11-12 | Anatomy, fit and raglan geometry, then your third project |
| 6 — Knitting on your own | 13-14 | Designing and writing patterns, and the habits of experienced knitters |

Each module ends with a scenario quiz: eight to ten situations from that module's material. The
pass mark is 70%.

## A note on time

Reading is about 10-12 hours in total. Knitting is 50-80 hours, and that is the honest number:
a sweater is a slow, restful thing, not a weekend. The course is built so that you get a finished
swatch within the first hour and a finished scarf within about two weeks of a few short sessions a
week. Every practice swatch counts, and every one of them is a step toward the next project.
