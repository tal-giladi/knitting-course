# Glossary

Knitting terms used in this course, with the British name in brackets where it differs. The course
uses the American spelling in the lessons; both are common, and a pattern written in the other
language is not wrong.

Terms are listed roughly in the order you meet them, each with the lesson that introduces it.

## A

**Aran** — a yarn weight between worsted and bulky, the classic sweater yarn. Introduced in 06.3.

## B

**Back loop** — the half of a stitch loop at the back of the needle, away from you. Knitting
through the back loop by accident makes a twisted stitch. Introduced in 02.2.

**Bind off** (UK: *cast off*) — taking the live loops off the needle and closing them so the fabric
cannot unravel. Introduced in 02.4.

**Block / blocking** — wetting a finished piece and drying it flat, so the fibres relax into the
shape the knitting wants to take. Introduced in 04.3.

**Bust** — the chest area of a garment; the measurement a sweater is sized by. Introduced in 11.1.

## C

**Cast on** — putting the first row of live loops onto the needle. The cast-on is *not* extra
fabric: it is the bottom edge, and it can be taken off again. Introduced in 01.5.

**CCW / CW** — counterclockwise and clockwise, the directions yarn is wound onto a ball or a skein.

**Chart** — a picture of a stitch pattern, where one square is one stitch. Introduced in 10.3.

**Continental knitting** (also *picking*) — holding the yarn in the left hand and picking it up with
the needle from the front. Introduced in 01.4.

**Count** — the stitch and row tally at the end of a row or section. Experienced knitters count out
loud; it is the single habit that prevents most mistakes. Introduced in 14.2.

**Cowl** — a wide, short tube worn around the neck. A short scarf makes the same object and needs
no shaping.

## D

**Decrease** — a way of removing one stitch from the fabric so it gets narrower. Introduced in 07.3.

**DPNs** — double-pointed needles, short needles with a point at both ends, an alternative to a
circular needle. Introduced in 08.1.

**Dropped stitch** — a stitch that has slipped off the needle but is still attached below by its
legs. It looks like a hole with a ladder of bars above it. Introduced in 05.4.

## E

**Ease** — the difference between your body measurement and the garment's finished measurement.
Positive ease is roomier, negative ease is snugger. A hat wants negative ease; a sweater usually
wants a little positive ease. Introduced in 11.1.

## F

**Fibre / fiber** — the material a yarn is made of (wool, cotton, acrylic). Introduced in 06.4.

**Frog / frogging** — deliberately unravelling a mistake back to a safe point. Named after a friend
who was a very enthusiastic ripper. Introduced in 05.5.

## G

**Gauge** (UK: *tension*) — the number of stitches and rows in 10 cm (4"), and therefore the single
number that decides how big anything you knit will be. Introduced in 06.1.

**Garter stitch** — knitting every row, which makes ridges. It lies flat and never curls.
Introduced in 02.3.

## H

**Held stitches** — stitches taken off the needle and kept safe on a spare needle or a thread while
you work the rest of the piece. Introduced in 12.3.

## I

**Increase** — a way of adding one stitch so the fabric gets wider. Introduced in 07.2.

**In the round** — knitting in a tube, without turning the work, so there is no side seam.
Introduced in 08.1.

## J

**Jog** — the visible spiral staircase where the first and last stitch of a round meet. Introduced
in 08.4.

## K

**k2tog** — "knit two together", a decrease that leans to the right. Introduced in 07.3.

**KFB** — "knit front and back", an increase that makes one stitch into two. Introduced in 07.2.

**Knit** — the stitch that makes a `V` on the side facing you. Introduced in 02.1.

## L

**Ladder** — the run of loose horizontal bars left behind when a stitch has dropped out. Introduced
in 05.4.

**Leader** — the yarn running from the ball or skein to the work, under tension.

**Lifeline** — a contrasting thread run through the live stitches, so a mistake further down can be
unravelled back to a known-good point instead of to the cast-on. Introduced in 05.5.

## M

**Magic loop** — working a tube on a single circular needle by turning the cable inside out, one
half at a time. One needle, any size tube. Introduced in 08.1.

**Moss stitch** (also *seed stitch*) — alternating one knit and one purl, in both directions. Flat,
stretchy and textured. Introduced in 03.4.

## N

**Needle size** — the thickness of a needle, which controls how loose the fabric is. A bigger needle
gives more stitches and flatter fabric. Introduced in 06.3.

## P

**Ply** — one of the strands twisted together to make a yarn. A 4-ply yarn is four strands.
Introduced in 06.4.

**Purl** — the back of a knit stitch. It makes a bump on the side facing you. Introduced in 03.1.

## R

**Raglan** — a sleeve style where the shoulder seams run from the neckband to the underarm, and the
sleeve grows by increases on both sides of four lines. Introduced in 11.3.

**Repeat** — a short sequence of stitches worked over and over across a row or round. Introduced
in 10.2.

**Ribbing** — alternating columns of knit and purl, which makes fabric that is both elastic and
flat. Introduced in 03.3.

**RS / WS** — right side and wrong side of the fabric. The right side is the side where knits make
`V`s when the piece is worked back and forth.

## S

**Schematic** — a drawing of a garment's shape with measurements, showing how the pieces fit
together. Introduced in 10.4.

**Selvedge** (UK: *selvedge*, US also *selvage*) — the side edges of a piece worked flat, where the
first and last stitches of each row are.

**Skein** — yarn wound into a long loop, ready to knit from, usually with a label.

**Slip knot** — the adjustable loop you start a cast-on with. Introduced in 01.5.

**ssk** — "slip slip knit", a decrease that leans to the left. Introduced in 07.3.

**Stockinette** — the plain fabric made by knitting one row and purling the next. Smooth and
beautiful, and it curls. Introduced in 03.2.

**Swatch** — a small piece knitted only to measure gauge or to compare fabrics. This course makes
one in almost every lesson.

## T

**Tink** — undoing one stitch at a time with the needles, the opposite of knitting, so a mistake can
be corrected without unravelling a whole row. Introduced in 05.3.

**Tension** — how firmly you hold the yarn. Consistent tension matters more than tight tension.
Introduced in 01.4.

**Top-down** — knitting a garment from the collar downward, so you can try it on as you go and
always know it will fit. Introduced in 11.2.

## W

**Wale** — a column of stitches, running vertically. The other axis of the fabric is the **course**,
a row.

**Wrong side** (WS) — the side of the fabric facing you when the work is inside out; the reverse of
the right side.

## Y

**Yarn over** (yo) — laying the yarn over the needle to make a new loop. A deliberate yarn-over
makes an increase; an accidental one is a hole. Introduced in 07.2.

**Yoke** — the top part of a top-down sweater, worked before the sleeves and body are separated.
Introduced in 11.3.

**Yarn weight** — the thickness category of a yarn (lace, sock, sport, DK, worsted, aran, bulky,
super bulky). Introduced in 06.3.
