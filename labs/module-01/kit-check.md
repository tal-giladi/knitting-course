# Kit check

Do this once, at the start, before your first stitch. It takes five minutes and it prevents the
single most common beginner problem: yarn and needles that are too hard to see.

## 1 · The needle

| | |
|---|---|
| Type | circular, with a cable |
| Cable length | ______ cm (you want 80 cm / 32") |
| Tip size printed on the needle | ______ mm |
| US size (5 mm = US 8) | ______ |
| Both tips are the same size? | yes / no |

If your needle is a different size, that is completely fine — note it and carry on. The course
assumes 5 mm (US 8) so that every number in the lessons has one answer, and every project fits the
same pattern. If you use a different size, you will still make everything; you will just get a
different gauge, and the course teaches you how to correct for that in Module 6.

## 2 · The yarn

| | Check |
|---|---|
| Weight | worsted / aran, or a similar medium yarn |
| Fibre | wool, or a wool blend |
| Colour | light and plain, not dark and not variegated |
| Texture | smooth — not fluffy, not mohair, not very slippery |
| How much | one ball is enough to start |

**The visibility test.** Wind about 20 m of the yarn and hold it against a plain background in
daylight. If you cannot see individual strands at arm's length, it will be hard work with it. A
beginner yarn is a *teaching* choice, not a budget choice: you will be pulling loose strands back
into place constantly, and smooth light yarn is what makes that possible.

## 3 · The small extras

| Item | Have it? | Substitutes that work |
|---|---|---|
| Scissors | | a knife, or tear the yarn carefully |
| Blunt needle (tapestry needle) | | a sewing needle with a blunt tip; a knitting needle with a stopper |
| Tape measure | | a ruler, or a piece of string you lay down and measure |
| Two stitch markers | | two small loops of scrap yarn, or two small safety pins |
| A crochet hook, about 5 mm | | optional; makes fixing dropped stitches much easier |

> [!TIP]
> Everything on this list is either a household item or free. The course never asks you to buy
> anything else at any point.

## 4 · Comfort

Before you start, and while you are working:

- Sit with your back supported. The work can rest on a cushion or a table; it does not need to be
  held up in the air.
- Your shoulders should be down. If they creep up towards your ears, put the work down for ten
  seconds and drop them.
- Keep your grip loose. If your hand is tired after ten minutes, you are holding the yarn too hard,
  not knitting too long.
- Take a break every 25-30 minutes, even when you are in the middle of a row. Stop, shake your
  hands out, look at something far away for twenty seconds, then start again.
- Stop if anything actually hurts. Tingling fingers, numbness or pain in a thumb, wrist or elbow is
  a signal to stop for the day, not to push through. Come back tomorrow.
